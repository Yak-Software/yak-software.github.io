Small language models, used the right way

George Agapov · Yak Software

Used the right way, a small language model is as capable as a big one.

We tested this on a prototype financial advisor: a bank's loan and mortgage products, wrapped as a WhatsApp contact that customers can write to or call. It answers questions like whether a home is affordable, what the monthly payment would be, or how long to save for a deposit.

The idea is to give the model as little to do as possible. It does two narrow jobs on every turn — work out what the customer is asking, and read the facts out of their message — and deterministic software does the rest.

The conversation is driven by software, and the reply is fully deterministic. The model never writes a sentence the customer sees and never produces a number; a calculator computes every figure and a template phrases it. A wrong reading can only send the turn down another path the designer planned for.

The small model's own probabilities are a good guide to its mistakes. Sending only the turns it is unsure about to Claude Opus 5.5 keeps most of the cost and latency advantage and closes about a third of the gap.

Why this architecture

PredictableThe same message in the same state always gets the same answer. Every figure comes from a calculator and every sentence from a template, so what the customer sees can be reviewed, tested and signed off in advance — which is what a calculation or a commitment needs.
AccurateTwo narrow jobs with strict outputs are where a small fine-tuned model is strongest. It does not have to be eloquent, only right about what the customer asked and what they said.
It cannot be talked into anythingThe bot answers only in the ways its designer wrote. There is no free-text generation to steer, so a customer cannot prompt it into a promise, an off-topic answer or a wrong number. A misreading can only choose another planned path, which then asks the customer to confirm.
Cheap, fast and in-houseThe whole kit — speech recognition, speech synthesis, the decision engine and every calculator — runs on a single server with a 24 GB GPU, and that one server already serves many conversations at once. Nothing leaves the building, and latency is ours to control: it beats many larger hosted models.
