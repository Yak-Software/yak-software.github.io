Two lines of work with one aim: AI that can be trusted where a mistake is expensive.

Proof-driven development

AI agents change the economics of formal verification. Proof search, following type errors and repairing proofs after a change become cheap, and the trust stays with the proof checker.

For a compact property that must never be violated, a machine-checked proof about the code that ships can now be part of normal development. Tests stay the better tool for behaviour, integration and performance.

The first substantial result is Molt Petit: a Rust validator imported into Lean 4, with its light-client safety proved in about fifteen thousand lines of Lean.

Small language models, used the right way

Used the right way, a small language model is as capable as a big one. On a prototype that wraps a bank's loan and mortgage products as a WhatsApp contact, a fine-tuned 4B model does two narrow jobs — work out what the customer is asking, and read the facts out of their message — and deterministic software does the rest.

Every reply is deterministic: calculators produce the figures and templates the sentences, so the bot answers only in the ways its designer wrote. The whole kit, speech in and out included, runs on one server with a 24 GB GPU.
